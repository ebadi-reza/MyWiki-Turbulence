# Thread 6 — Wind-tunnel ramp (apparatus)

Construction of a **ramp installed in a wind tunnel** to create streamwise pressure-gradient (PG) conditions — apparatus/instrumentation work by Reza.

## Reza's contribution

- **The FPF ramp** — **built by A. Ebadi (Reza) & C. J. Klewicki (J. Klewicki's son) at UNH** (per the acknowledgement in  [[2022-romero-jfm|Romero et al. 2022]], which is the published source for the data it enabled).
  Reza built the ramp; the data was collected afterward.
  - Installed in the **[[flow-physics-facility|Flow Physics Facility (FPF)]]** at UNH — a large ZPG wind tunnel, **2.8 m × 6 m** cross-section, **72 m fetch** (Vincenti et al. 2013). This is a **separate rig** from the [[neat-wind-tunnel|NEAT tunnel]] of [[2019-biles-jfe]].
  - **Design:** based on Aubertine & Eaton (2005), but installed on the **ceiling** (not the floor),
    so measurements on the flat floor capture **PG effects without wall curvature**.
  - **Geometry:** an initial **FPG** region (reduces tunnel height 0.4 m over 3.1 m) → a **ZPG flat**
    relaxation region (~7.0 m, flow returns to near-canonical) → an **APG ramp** (expands height
    0.5 m over 5.3 m) → **ZPG** development downstream (~20 m fetch). Ramp insert ~15 m long.
  - **Purpose:** generate controlled adverse/favorable pressure gradients to study non-equilibrium (PG) turbulent boundary layers at high Reynolds number.

## What it enabled

- **APG turbulent-boundary-layer measurements** — [[2022-romero-jfm]]: hot-wire measurements along  the APG ramp (δ⁺≈7100–7770, β≤1.8) probing the [[adverse-pressure-gradient-tbl|inertial sublayer]].
  See that source and [[adverse-pressure-gradient-tbl]] for the physics.
