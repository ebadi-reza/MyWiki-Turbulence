# Infrared (IR) thermography

**What it is.** Non-contact measurement of a surface's temperature field from its emitted infrared radiation, using an IR camera. Gives a full 2D map of wall temperature — useful for assessing spatial uniformity and for revealing flow structure via its thermal footprint.

## As used in Reza's work ([[2019-biles-jfe]])

- **Camera:** FLIR SC645 (spectral range 7.5–13 μm, 640×480 px, manufacturer uncertainty ±2% of the measured temperature). Imaging through BK7 glass windows in the tunnel test section.
- **Uses:** (1) verify the [[neat-wind-tunnel|thermal wall plate]] holds a uniform set-point temperature (spanwise variation < IR uncertainty); (2) image the wall-temperature footprint of 3D flow structures (e.g. the necklace vortex / wake behind a wall-mounted hemisphere), where cold freestream fluid brought to the wall marks high heat-transfer regions.
- Cross-checked against embedded J-thermocouples (±0.01 °C).

## Caveats

- Emissivity differences (e.g. between insulated gaps and the metal plate) make some regions
  unreliable; absolute accuracy limited by the camera (±2%).
