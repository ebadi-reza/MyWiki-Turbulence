# Flow Physics Facility (FPF) & the pressure-gradient ramp

**What it is.** The **Flow Physics Facility (FPF)** at the University of New Hampshire — a very large
ZPG wind tunnel used to reach **high Reynolds numbers**: cross-section **2.8 m × 6 m**, streamwise
**fetch ~72 m** (Vincenti et al. 2013). Distinct from the smaller [[neat-wind-tunnel|NEAT tunnel]].

## The pressure-gradient ramp

To impose a streamwise pressure gradient, a **ramp structure** (built by **A. Ebadi & C.J. Klewicki**
— J. Klewicki's son, not the author Joseph C. Klewicki; see [[wind-tunnel-ramp]]) was installed.
Design notes ([[2022-romero-jfm]]):

- Based on Aubertine & Eaton (2005), but mounted on the **ceiling** (not the floor), so measurements on the flat floor capture **PG effects without wall curvature**.
- Streamwise sequence: **FPG** (height −0.4 m over 3.1 m) → **ZPG relaxation** (~7.0 m) → **APG ramp** (height +0.5 m over 5.3 m) → **ZPG** development (~20 m fetch). Insert ~15 m long.
- Pressure measured on the floor via Pitot-static (10 cm above floor); `C_p` distribution matches
  Aubertine & Eaton (2005) apart from suction peaks at surface discontinuities. APG influence spans `−0.5 ≲ x_scaled ≲ 1.6` (`x_scaled` normalized by the 5.3 m APG length).

## Why it matters

- Enables **high-Re, modest-β APG** boundary-layer measurements ([[2022-romero-jfm]]) — a *spatial* non-equilibrium complement to the *temporal* forcing of the [[neat-wind-tunnel|NEAT tunnel]].
- The long ZPG relaxation section before the APG ramp gives a cleaner near-canonical inflow than many prior ramp experiments.
